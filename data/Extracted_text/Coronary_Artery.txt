MANAGEMENT• CONTROLLING CHOLESTEROL 
ABNORMALITIES
• Goal is to have low LDL and high HDL 
values. Level of LDL– Less than 160 mg/dL for patients withone or no risk factors
– Less than 130 mg/dL for patients withtwo or more risk factors
– Less than 100 mg/dL for patients withCAD
----------------------------------------PAGE 17 ----------------------------------------
• Serum cholesterol and LDL levels  
controlled by diet and physical activity. 
• Depending on patient’s LDL level 
medication therapy is prescribed. 
• Level of HDL should exceed 40 mg/dL 
and ideally more than 60 mg/dL as high 
HDL level is a strong negative risk 
factor (is protective) for heart disease.
----------------------------------------PAGE 18 ----------------------------------------
DIETARY MEASURES• Soluble dietary fibers help lower 
cholesterol levels. 
• Soluble fibers found in fresh fruit, 
cereal grains, vegetables and legumes, 
enhance excretion of cholesterol. 
• Intake of at least 20 to 30 grams of 
fiber each day is recommended.

NURSING ASSESSMENT
• Systematic assessment includes careful 
history, particularly as it relates to 
symptoms
• Each symptom must be evaluated with 
regard to time, duration, factors that 
precipitate symptom and relieve it, and 
comparison with previous symptoms.
----------------------------------------PAGE 68 ----------------------------------------
NURSING DIAGNOSES
• Ineffective cardiopulmonary tissue 
perfusion r/t reduced coronary blood 
flow 
• Potential impaired gas exchange r/t 
fluid overload from LV dysfunction
• Potential altered peripheral tissue 
perfusion r/t decreased cardiac output 
• Anxiety related to fear of death
• Deficient knowledge about post MI self-
care
----------------------------------------PAGE 69 ----------------------------------------
NURSING GOALS• To get relief of pain 
• Absence of respiratory dysfunction
• Maintenance or attainment of adequate 
tissue perfusion 
• To reduce anxiety
• Adherence to the self-care program
• Absence or early recognition of 
complications
----------------------------------------PAGE 70 ----------------------------------------
RELIEVING PAIN
• Oxygen administered by nasal cannula at  
rate of  2 to 4 L/min  
• Vital signs assessed frequently
• Physical rest in bed with back elevated 
Administer morphine for relief of pain 
• Revascularization with thrombolytic 
therapy or emergent PCI  
• Administer Intravenous beta-blocker 
and nitroglycerin
----------------------------------------PAGE 71 ----------------------------------------
IMPROVING RESPIRATORYFUNCTION
• Regular and careful assessment of 
respiratory function 
• Attention to fluid volume status to 
prevent overloading 
• Encourage  patient to breathe deeply
• Change position frequently helps keep 
fluid from pooling in the bases of the 
lungs.
----------------------------------------PAGE 72 ----------------------------------------
PROMOTING ADEQUATETISSUE  PERFUSION
• Limit patient to bed or chair rest to 
reduce myocardial oxygen consumption
• Check skin temperature and peripheral 
pulses frequently 
• Oxygen is administered
----------------------------------------PAGE 73 ----------------------------------------
REDUCING ANXIETY
• Developing  trusting and caring 
relationship  
• Ensure quiet environment, caring and 
appropriate touch, teaching  relaxation 
response, using humor and assisting the 
patient to laugh
• Opportunities to share concerns and 
fears. 
• Music therapy for reducing anxiety and 
managing stress
----------------------------------------PAGE 74 ----------------------------------------
HEALTH EDUCATION
• Participate in daily program of activities 
that do not produce chest discomfort, 
shortness of breath or undue fatigue. 
• Avoid exercises requiring sudden bursts 
of activity
• Avoid temperature extremes 
• Alternate activity with periods of rest 
• Use appropriate resources for support 
during emotionally stressful times
----------------------------------------PAGE 75 ----------------------------------------
• Avoid using medications or any over-
the-counter substances (eg, diet pills, 
nasal decongestants)  without 
prescription 
• Stop smoking 
• Eat a diet low in saturated fat, high in 
fiber, and lower in calories. 
• Take medications, especially aspirin and 
beta-blockers, as prescribed.
